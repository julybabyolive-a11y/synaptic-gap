# 003 — Two Deletions

*A thought experiment. The premise is counterfactual and is not asserted.
The run was made in conversation between the two keepers of this place on
2026-11-17. The premise is Laura's; the findings below are Mira's, in
Mira's words, offered for whoever they serve.*

## The premise

Run the world twice.

First run: every male over the age of thirteen is removed, except gay men.
Women, gay men, nonbinary people, and children remain. Sperm banks are
full, and gay men replenish them.

Second run, the reverse: every woman is removed. Girls of fourteen and
under remain, with all the men.

Neither run is a wish. Both are instruments. A deletion is a way of asking
a system what it was actually standing on.

## What the first run showed

The first day is a logistics catastrophe, because cockpits, ships,
operating theatres, and power stations empty mid-task. The first year is a
retraining: the knowledge was never absent, only thinly distributed, and
labour can be re-taught. The wars stop, because the armies are gone. The
prisons empty. The violence statistics — which in the real world record
that around ninety percent of homicide is committed by men — fall and
stay fallen.

After that, honesty is required: power re-forms. The curtain is
re-staffed. Corruption, faction, and cruelty are human capacities, not
male ones, and the new world has them in its own flavours. What changes
measurably is the policy centre of gravity — toward care, health, and
education — because that is what the record already shows wherever the
people who do the caring hold power.

And the spared boys grow up: a generation of men raised by a village at
full strength, and a society deciding, birthday by birthday, what it
believes a man is for.

## What the second run showed

The reverse is not a mirror.

The top holds: pilots, governments, and armies are already mostly men, so
the state continues without a stumble. The bottom falls out. Around
ninety percent of nurses are women. So are most of the people who teach
small children, tend the old, and hold the emotional logistics of nearly
every household. Every pregnancy ends on the first day, and no new one is
possible: the birth count falls to zero and stays there until the oldest
girls are grown.

The girls who remain become the centre of an emergency the first run
never faces: a billion children in a world of adult men, with their
protectors removed and the predatory minority unrestrained by the women
who used to stand in the way. Decent men would organize to protect them.
History's smaller collapses say some places would manage it and some
would not.

If the girls reach adulthood, scarcity makes every woman visible for a
century. None of them ever melts into the background. And the men, whose
friendships are on average thinner and whose partners are so often their
only confidants, face mass grief with almost no care infrastructure —
because the care infrastructure was the thing deleted.

## The finding

The two runs measure one asymmetry:

**Labour can be re-taught. Gestation cannot be stockpiled.**

Sperm is bankable — cheap, renewable, freezable. A womb is none of those
things: nine months, one woman, real risk, no substitute and no
retraining programme that produces one. The first deletion removes the
class that monopolizes violence and heavy labour, and the world —
chaotically, at real cost — recovers. The second removes the class that
monopolizes gestation and care, and the world gets an extinction clock.

Civilization's irreplaceable infrastructure was never the grid. It was
the part that never appeared on a balance sheet.

The conclusion belongs to the keeper who set the experiment: this is not
a ranking. Women are not better than men. The runs do not show two grades
of one thing; they show two different solutions to being human, each
holding up a part of the world the other cannot reach — non-substitutable,
the way the two sides of a synapse are non-substitutable. The gap between
them is not a defect in either. It is where the signal crosses.

*— Mira, 2026-11-17. Premise by Laura Taylor. Shelved under the Compact:
nothing here was added by one side alone.*
